# Opération Migration du Poulet de Feu — Drive-Home Emergency Preparedness Checklist

**Vehicle**: 1968 Pontiac Firebird, period-correct Pontiac 400 V8 (points ignition assumed from engine bay photo). Storage history per seller (the JUN '21 decal on the plate is the last sticker Alberta issued before abolishing the program in 2018 — it does **not** indicate the car has been off-road; confirm actual usage with the seller).
**Route**: Alberta → BC → Pacific Highway crossing → I-5 south → Seattle (~1,200-1,500 km / 750-950 mi)
**Duration**: 2-3 days driving; longer if you stage near the seller and the border

A 58-year-old car parked for 5 years has a meaningful failure rate per 100 miles. The principle here is **prevention > spares > flatbed**: replace consumables before departure (so they don't fail), carry a small kit for things you can actually fix in a parking lot (~30 minutes, daylight, basic tools), and let the Hagerty flatbed handle anything bigger. There is no scenario where you rebuild a carb on the shoulder of I-5.

---

## Tier 1 — Pre-Trip (Replace, Don't Carry)

A new belt in the engine bay beats a spare belt in the trunk. Refresh these in Alberta before crossing. Most are cheap; all are easier to do in a shop than on a roadside.

### Drive-and-inspect first

- [ ] **Drive 50-100 miles in mixed conditions** (city, highway, hills) before crossing. Any car parked 5 years will surface at least one issue in that window. Catch it where the seller can help.
- [ ] **Watch for**: leaks, overheating, charging issues, brake pull, transmission slip, ignition miss, fuel starvation under load.

### Replace before departure

- [ ] **Fan belt** (V-belt for water pump/alternator)
- [ ] **All coolant hoses** — upper rad, lower rad, heater — plus all clamps (replace clamps with stainless)
- [ ] **Coolant** — drain and fill with a known-fresh universal-compatible 50/50 (Prestone or equivalent)
- [ ] **Engine oil + filter** — weight per seller; if unknown, 10W-30 is the safe call
- [ ] **Transmission fluid** check (drain/fill if it smells burnt or looks brown)
- [ ] **Fuel** — run tank low, fill fresh, add Sta-Bil. Old ethanol gas is varnish risk
- [ ] **Fuel filter** (inline)
- [ ] **Ignition refresh** — points, condenser, cap, rotor, spark plugs (8), plug wires (full set, pre-cut for 400 V8)
- [ ] **Battery** — load-test; replace if marginal. Clean and tighten terminals
- [ ] **Tires** — replace if older than 6-7 years (DOT date code on sidewall, last 4 digits = week/year), regardless of tread. Confirm spare matches Magnum 500 lug pattern
- [ ] **Brake fluid** — top up; bleed if pedal feels spongy
- [ ] **All lights** — head, tail, brake, signals, hazards. Vintage wiring is the #1 inspection failure
- [ ] **Wipers** — replace blades if borderline
- [ ] **Carb tune** — idle, mixture, choke. Edelbrock carbs are well-documented for self-service

## Tier 2 — In the Trunk (Compact Kit)

The whole kit fits in one ~30L tote plus a small duffel for personal/safety. Pack heavy items low and centered, fluids upright in a waterproof tray.

### Spares (the few worth carrying)

- [ ] **Points + condenser** spare set — tiny, fail without warning
- [ ] **Cap + rotor** spare — small, cheap insurance after replacement
- [ ] **Fuses** — assortment for the original spec (1968 GM uses SFE-type glass cylinder; verify and bring SFE 4, 6, 9, 14, 20, 30)
- [ ] **Hose clamps** — assortment, stainless, sizes #10-#28
- [ ] **Heater hose** — 5/8" universal, 3-4 feet (patches a lot of things)
- [ ] **Self-fusing silicone tape** (Rescue Tape) — best emergency hose patch

### Fluids (top-up only, not full refills)

- [ ] **Coolant** — 1 gal pre-mix
- [ ] **Distilled water** — 1 gal (cooling top-up; never tap water)
- [ ] **Engine oil** — 2 qt of the weight currently in the car
- [ ] **ATF** — 1 qt Dexron-III/VI
- [ ] **Brake fluid** — small bottle, DOT 3
- [ ] **Carb cleaner** spray
- [ ] **PB Blaster** or Liquid Wrench

### Tools (compact set — fits in one roll)

- [ ] **3/8" drive socket set, SAE only**, sizes 7/16" through 3/4" (~90% of GM fasteners)
- [ ] **Combination wrenches SAE**: 7/16, 1/2, 9/16, 5/8, 3/4
- [ ] **Adjustable wrench** — 10"
- [ ] **Pliers** — regular, needle-nose, channel-lock, vise-grip locking
- [ ] **Screwdrivers** — Phillips #2, flat 1/4"
- [ ] **Multimeter** — DC volts, continuity ($30, any auto parts store)
- [ ] **Headlamp** — hands-free LED
- [ ] **Bailing wire** — 16-gauge stainless
- [ ] **Electrical tape** (3M Super 33+)
- [ ] **Duct tape** (Gorilla)
- [ ] **Zip ties** — assortment incl. 12" heavy-duty
- [ ] **Tire plug kit** + **12V inflator**
- [ ] **Lug wrench** — confirm matches Magnum 500 lug nuts (some need a different socket size than OEM)
- [ ] **Funnel** — flexible spout for ATF
- [ ] **Shop rags** — small bundle

### Safety

- [ ] **Lithium jump pack** (NOCO Boost or similar) — replaces jumper cables, doubles as USB charger
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher** — B/C-rated, halotron or non-corrosive (NOT dry chemical near electronics or carbs). Cabin-mount, not trunk
- [ ] **First aid kit** — comprehensive, plus any personal meds
- [ ] **Reflective triangles** (3-pack) or **LED flares**
- [ ] **Reflective vest**
- [ ] **Tow strap** — 15-20 ft, 10,000+ lb, looped ends

### Personal

- [ ] **Drinking water** — 1 gallon
- [ ] **Snacks** — 1 day's worth
- [ ] **Wool blanket** OR sleeping bag (pick one)
- [ ] **Rain jacket** + warm layer + sturdy boots
- [ ] **Phone car charger** (12V, USB-A/C splitter — verify cigarette lighter works pre-trip)
- [ ] **Power bank** — 20,000 mAh+
- [ ] **Cash** — CAD 200, USD 200 (rural shops, towing)

### Documentation (in a binder, in the cabin)

- [ ] **Insurance binder card + declarations page** (Hagerty/ACI/Grundy)
- [ ] **Roadside number** written somewhere visible without unlocking the car (visor)
- [ ] **CAA / AAA card** if active (CAA covers Canadian side; AAA reciprocates US)
- [ ] **Phone numbers**: insurance roadside, seller, a Pontiac-knowledgeable friend, CBSA Pacific Highway, CBP Blaine
- [ ] **Paper road atlas** for AB/BC/WA (cell gaps in northern BC and Cascades)
- [ ] **Saved offline maps** for the route

## Tier 3 — Skip / Call a Flatbed

Don't carry these. Don't try to fix these. The Hagerty Drivers Club Plus flatbed-to-shop-of-choice is the best feature of the policy — use it.

| Failure | Why you don't fix it roadside |
|---|---|
| Voltage regulator, alternator | Vintage units are failure-prone, but parking-lot diagnosis + swap is impractical. Flatbed |
| Fuel pump (mechanical) | Possible to swap roadside but miserable; one bad gasket and you're chasing a fuel smell for 500 miles |
| Carb rebuild | You will not do this on the side of the road |
| U-joint / driveshaft | Disabling, rare, flatbed |
| Transmission slip / no drive | Flatbed |
| Head gasket (white smoke, coolant in oil) | Flatbed |
| Timing chain (engine won't turn or clatters) | Flatbed |
| Bottom-end knock, bent valve | Flatbed |
| Carb fire / fuel leak | Stop, extinguish if safe, flatbed |
| Anything you can't diagnose in 30 minutes | Flatbed |

## When to Stop, When to Push, When to Tow

| Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|
| Temp gauge past 220°F | Stop ASAP, let cool, check coolant. Never pour cold water in a hot engine |
| Charging warning (gauge or light) | Pull over within 20 miles, check belt + battery voltage. On a points car, alternator-only kills the battery in 30-60 min |
| Engine miss / stumble | Ignition first (wires, cap, points). Carb second (clogged jet) |
| Oil pressure drops | Stop immediately. Pontiac V8 cam/lifters are the first to die, then bearings |
| Trans slip or burnt smell | Stop, let cool, check fluid. Flatbed for the rest of the trip |
| Smoke (any color) | Stop ASAP. White = coolant. Blue = oil. Black = fuel. Each is a different story |
| Burning smell, no smoke | Pull over, hand on hood/firewall to localize heat |

## Day-of-Departure Checklist

15 minutes well spent the morning of the drive.

- [ ] All fluids checked
- [ ] Tire pressure all 4 + spare (cold)
- [ ] All lights working (walkaround with someone for brake/signal)
- [ ] Battery terminals clean and tight
- [ ] Fuel topped off (Canadian gas usually cheaper after FX)
- [ ] All documents in binder (border + Bill of Sale + permit + insurance)
- [ ] Phone + power bank fully charged
- [ ] Tools and kit secured (won't shift on hard braking)
- [ ] Emergency contacts visible (visor or glove box)
- [ ] Trip permit displayed
- [ ] Walk around — anything dripping or loose?

## The Six Things You'll Be Glad You Brought

1. Lithium jump pack
2. Fire extinguisher
3. First aid kit
4. Cash
5. Roadside number written somewhere visible
6. The blanket

If you cut everything else, keep these six. Everything else in Tier 2 is the difference between a 30-minute roadside fix and a 4-hour wait for a flatbed — useful, but not life-or-death.
