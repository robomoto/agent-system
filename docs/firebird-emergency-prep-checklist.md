# Firebird Drive-Home Emergency Preparedness Checklist

**Vehicle**: 1968 Pontiac Firebird, period-correct Pontiac 400 V8 (points ignition assumed from engine bay photo), off-road since June 2021
**Route**: Alberta → BC → Pacific Highway crossing → I-5 south → Seattle (~1,200-1,500 km / 750-950 mi)
**Duration**: 2-3 days driving; longer if you stage near the seller and the border

A 58-year-old car that's been parked for 5 years has a meaningful failure-rate per 100 miles. The list below is calibrated to this car specifically — points ignition, mechanical fuel pump, no OBD-II, parts stores along I-5 stock generic GM but not always Pontiac-specific. The goal is roadside-fixable for the most common failures (cooling, ignition, fuel, electrical) and a clean handoff to a flatbed for anything bigger.

---

## Pre-Trip Shake-Down (do this BEFORE crossing the border)

A car off-road for 5 years will have **at least one** issue surface in the first 50 miles. Catch them in Alberta where the seller can help, not in Idaho on a Sunday.

- [ ] Drive 50-100 miles in mixed conditions (city, highway, hills) before crossing. Watch for leaks, overheating, charging issues, brake pull, transmission slip, ignition miss
- [ ] Check all fluids cold AND warm — engine oil, transmission, coolant, brake, power steering
- [ ] Check tire pressure cold and confirm tires aren't dry-rotted (sidewall cracks, weather checking). **Tires older than 6-8 years should be replaced before the trip**, regardless of tread depth — date code is on the sidewall (DOT XXXX format, last 4 digits = week/year)
- [ ] Test all lights (head, tail, brake, signals, hazards, dome) — vintage wiring is the #1 reason for inspection failures
- [ ] Test the horn (required for legal road use, often forgotten)
- [ ] Test wipers (rubber dries out; replace blades if borderline)
- [ ] Confirm seat belts function (mandatory for federal FMVSS exemption to apply on the WA side)
- [ ] Charge the battery overnight before the trip; load-test it if possible (vintage 12V batteries often hold charge but die under load)
- [ ] Run the gas tank down and **fill with fresh fuel** (5-year-old gas with 10% ethanol is a varnish/water risk). Add a fuel stabilizer like Sta-Bil for the journey.
- [ ] If the car has been sitting, **plan on a carb tune** before departure — idle adjust, mixture, choke. Edelbrock carbs are well-documented for self-service.

## Spares & Consumables (in the trunk, organized by category)

### Ignition & Electrical (highest failure rate on points-era cars)

- [ ] **Distributor cap** (one new spare)
- [ ] **Rotor** (one new spare)
- [ ] **Points + condenser** set (cheap insurance — points era cars run a long time on one set but they fail without warning)
- [ ] **Coil** (one spare — match voltage rating; original equipment usually 12V w/ ballast resistor, internal ohm spec matters)
- [ ] **Ballast resistor** if so equipped (1968 Pontiac typically has external ballast)
- [ ] **Spark plugs** — one full set (8) plus 1-2 extras
- [ ] **Spark plug wires** — one full set, pre-cut for the 400 V8 (have a spare set rather than cutting roadside)
- [ ] **Ignition coil** spare
- [ ] **Voltage regulator** — 1968 Pontiac uses external mechanical or solid-state regulator; vintage units are notoriously failure-prone
- [ ] **Fuses** — assortment matching original spec (1968 GM uses **SFE-type glass cylinder fuses** unless converted; bring SFE 4, 6, 9, 14, 20, 30; verify the fuse panel before buying)
- [ ] **Spare battery cables** (10ga or heavier, 4-6 ft each, with terminal ends)
- [ ] **Battery terminal cleaner** (wire brush)
- [ ] **Dielectric grease** (for spark plug boots and connector pins)

### Cooling System

- [ ] **Fan belt** — Pontiac 400 typically uses a single V-belt for water pump/alternator; bring 2 spares (length specific — measure or have seller confirm). A failed belt is a 30-minute roadside fix; a missing spare is a flatbed
- [ ] **Upper radiator hose** + clamp (Pontiac 400-specific sizing — get from rockauto.com or Year One pre-trip; generic store may not have it)
- [ ] **Lower radiator hose** + clamp
- [ ] **Heater hose** — 5/8" universal heater hose, a few feet
- [ ] **Hose clamps** — assortment, all-stainless, sizes #10 through #28
- [ ] **Coolant** — pre-mixed 50/50 (if you don't know what's in the car, use a universal-compatible like Prestone; mixing types can cause sludge)
- [ ] **Distilled water** — 2 gallons (for top-up; never use tap water in the cooling system)
- [ ] **Thermostat** spare (cheap)
- [ ] **Stop-leak** pellets (last-resort emergency only; can clog the heater core if overused — keep in the box)

### Fuel System

- [ ] **Mechanical fuel pump** spare (1968 Pontiac uses a mechanical pump on the block; aftermarket Edelbrock fuel pumps may differ — verify what's installed)
- [ ] **Inline fuel filter** spare
- [ ] **Fuel hose** — short length of high-pressure rubber line, sized to the carb inlet (typically 5/16" or 3/8")
- [ ] **Carb cleaner** spray
- [ ] **Fuel stabilizer** (Sta-Bil or equivalent — already added at fill-up, bring more for top-up)
- [ ] **Spare carb rebuild kit** if you know the carb model (Edelbrock kits are inexpensive insurance — but only useful if you can rebuild roadside, which is probably impractical; skip if not confident)

### Engine

- [ ] **Engine oil** — 4-6 quarts of the weight currently in the car (ask seller; likely 10W-30 or 20W-50 for an old Pontiac)
- [ ] **ATF** — Dexron-III or Dexron-VI (TH350/TH400 takes either) — 2 quarts
- [ ] **Brake fluid** — DOT 3 (DOT 4 is OK, do NOT mix DOT 5 silicone with conventional)
- [ ] **Power steering fluid** if equipped (Pontiac PS pumps usually take ATF, but verify the cap label)
- [ ] **PB Blaster** or **Liquid Wrench** (penetrating oil)
- [ ] **WD-40** (general purpose)

### Driveline / Transmission

- [ ] **Universal joint** spare (driveshaft U-joint failure is rare but disabling — flatbed if it goes)
- [ ] No spare needed for the auto trans itself; if it fails, it's a flatbed

## Tools

Pack these in a structured tool roll or rigid case. Soft rolls protect against rattle but rigid cases organize better for a long road trip.

### Hand tools

- [ ] **Socket set** (1/4", 3/8", 1/2" drive — SAE/imperial sizes 1/4" through 1-1/8"; metric NOT needed for a US car)
- [ ] **Combination wrenches** SAE — full set 1/4" through 1"
- [ ] **Adjustable wrench** — 10" and 6"
- [ ] **Pliers** — regular, needle-nose, channel-lock (Channellock 420 or 440), vise-grip locking pliers
- [ ] **Screwdrivers** — Phillips #1, #2, #3 and flat 1/8", 1/4", 5/16"
- [ ] **Allen / hex key set** (Edelbrock fittings often use hex)
- [ ] **Pliers — wire strippers / crimpers** (for electrical)
- [ ] **Diagonal cutters / dikes**
- [ ] **Ratcheting wrench set** (worth the weight — speeds bolt changes by 3x)

### Diagnostic / electrical

- [ ] **Multimeter** (DC volts, ohms, continuity — $30 from any auto parts store)
- [ ] **Test light** (12V probe with ground clip — fastest way to trace power)
- [ ] **Inspection mirror** (extendable, with light)
- [ ] **Magnetic pickup** (extendable — for retrieving dropped fasteners)

### Tire / road

- [ ] **Floor jack** or hydraulic bottle jack (the OEM scissor jack is fine for a tire change but inadequate for any other work)
- [ ] **Jack stands** — pair, 3-ton minimum
- [ ] **Lug wrench** — confirm matches the Magnum 500 wheels' lug nuts (some aftermarket wheels need a different socket size than the OEM Pontiac wrench)
- [ ] **Tire pressure gauge** — pencil or digital
- [ ] **12V tire inflator / compressor** (Slime, Viair, etc.)
- [ ] **Tire plug kit** (mushroom or string-style)
- [ ] **Spare tire** — confirm condition AND that it matches the Magnum 500 lug pattern/offset

### Misc

- [ ] **Funnel set** (for fluids — including a flexible pour spout for ATF)
- [ ] **Drain pan** or oil-soak mat (compact, foldable)
- [ ] **Mechanic's gloves** + **nitrile / latex gloves**
- [ ] **Shop rags** — bag of 25
- [ ] **Paper towels** — heavy-duty
- [ ] **Trash bags** (for the rags after, and any fluid spills)
- [ ] **Headlamp** — hands-free LED (Black Diamond or similar)
- [ ] **Flashlight** — handheld backup (Maglite or LED equivalent)
- [ ] **Bailing wire / mechanic's wire** (16-gauge stainless or galvanized — fixes a thousand things)
- [ ] **Electrical tape** (3M Super 33+, not the cheap dollar-store kind)
- [ ] **Duct tape** (Gorilla)
- [ ] **Self-fusing silicone tape** (Rescue Tape — better than duct for hose patches)
- [ ] **Zip ties** — assortment, including 12" heavy-duty
- [ ] **Bungee cords** (3-4)
- [ ] **Ratchet strap** (1-2, 10-15 ft, 1500+ lb rated)
- [ ] **Tow strap** (15-20 ft, 10,000+ lb rated, looped ends — kinetic ropes are nice but optional)

## Safety & Road Equipment

- [ ] **Jumper cables** — 12-foot minimum, 4-gauge (anything thinner is theater for a V8). Or bring a **lithium jump pack** (NOCO Boost or similar) which doubles as a phone charger and works without a donor car
- [ ] **Reflective triangles** (3-pack) OR **road flares** (LED preferred; old chemical flares are a fire risk in a car with possible vapor leaks)
- [ ] **Reflective vest** (for if you're working roadside at night)
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher** — **B/C-rated, halotron or similar non-corrosive** (do NOT use dry chemical near electronics or carbs — corrosive residue requires teardown). Mount in the cabin, not the trunk
- [ ] **First aid kit** — comprehensive, including triangular bandage, burn gel, tweezers, scissors, gauze, ace wrap, and anything personal-medical (allergies, prescriptions)
- [ ] **Roadside whistle**
- [ ] **Glow sticks** (4-6, useful for marking the car at night)
- [ ] **Tire chains** if traveling through Coquihalla / Snoqualmie passes in shoulder season (mid-May usually clear, but verify pass conditions before departure: drivebc.ca, wsdot.com)

## Day-of-Departure Checklist

Run through this the morning of the drive. 15 minutes well spent.

- [ ] All fluids checked, topped if needed
- [ ] Tire pressure all 4 + spare (cold)
- [ ] All lights working (walk around with someone to confirm brake/signal)
- [ ] Battery terminals clean and tight
- [ ] Fuel topped off (last cheap-fuel opportunity — Canadian gas is often cheaper than WA after USD/CAD conversion)
- [ ] All documents in the binder (border + Bill of Sale + permit + insurance)
- [ ] Phone fully charged + power bank charged
- [ ] Tools and spares loaded and secured (won't shift on hard braking)
- [ ] Emergency contacts list visible (visor or glove box)
- [ ] Trip-permit displayed correctly
- [ ] Walk around the car — anything dripping? Anything loose?

## Mental Model — When to Stop, When to Push, When to Tow

| Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|
| Temperature gauge climbing past 220°F | Stop ASAP, let it cool, check coolant level. Do NOT pour cold water into a hot engine. |
| Charging system warning (gauge or light) | Pull over within 20 miles, check belt and battery voltage. Driving on alternator-only kills the battery in 30-60 min on a points car (ignition draw). |
| Engine miss / stumble | Check ignition first (wires, cap, points). Carb second (clogged jet). |
| Oil pressure drops | Stop immediately. Do not drive on a Pontiac V8 with low oil pressure — the cam and lifters are the first to die, then the bearings. |
| Trans slip / overheat smell | Stop, let cool, check fluid level. Consider flatbed for the rest of the trip. |
| Smoke from any source | Stop ASAP. Identify color: white = coolant, blue = oil, black = fuel. Each tells a different story. |
| Burning smell, no smoke yet | Pull over, walk around the car with a hand on hood/firewall to localize heat |
| Anything you can't diagnose in 30 min | Call the roadside number on your insurance and request a flatbed to your shop of choice in Seattle |

## Roadside Repair Won't-Fix List (just call a flatbed)

- Blown head gasket (white smoke, loss of compression, coolant in oil)
- Snapped timing chain (engine won't crank or makes a clatter)
- Broken transmission (slipping, no drive in any gear)
- Snapped axle / driveshaft U-joint (clunking + loss of drive)
- Bent valve / bottom-end knock
- Carb fire that took out wiring or fuel lines
- Anything involving fuel and fire

For all of these, the flatbed-to-shop-of-choice benefit on the Hagerty Drivers Club Plus is the single best feature of the policy. Use it.
