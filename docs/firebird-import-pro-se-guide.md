## 1. Before You Start

### What you are actually doing, in plain English

You are taking a 1968 Pontiac Firebird that lives in Alberta, Canada, and:

1. **Removing it from Canada** by reporting the export to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) at least 72 hours in advance and presenting yourself at the Pacific Highway truck crossing in BC. CBSA stamps the Alberta registration and releases the car from Canada.
2. **Bringing it into the United States** by presenting yourself at the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) booth at Blaine, Washington, filing three federal forms (HS-7, EPA 3520-1, and CBP Form 7501), and paying federal fees (nothing if your duty-free claim is accepted, plus a small Merchandise Processing Fee).
3. **Driving it home to Seattle** on a Washington trip permit you bought *before* you crossed.
4. **Getting a Washington VIN inspection** from the Washington State Patrol within the trip-permit window.
5. **Getting a Washington title and license plates** at a Seattle vehicle-licensing subagent, paying Seattle/King County **use tax** (the largest single cost of the entire trip).
6. **Insuring it** under a classic-collector policy bound effective 2026-05-15 — the day of the border crossing.

### Why timing matters

Several deadlines move with each other and a single missed step can cascade:

- The **CBSA 72-hour rule** is mandatory. Filing late means you arrive at the booth with paperwork CBSA cannot accept and they will turn you around. (Authority: CBSA Memorandum **D-20-1-1**, *Export Reporting*; *Customs Act* (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (2nd Supp.)), s. 95.)
- The **WA trip permit is valid for 3 days only** (RCW 46.16A.320; WAC 308-93-090) and **must be obtained before driving on Washington roads**. If your VIN inspection or DOL appointment slips outside the 3-day window, you must buy a second permit. WA allows up to three permits per vehicle per year.
- The **WSP VIN inspection runs on a 2-week rolling appointment calendar** (https://wsp.wa.gov/driver/schedule-a-vin/) and slots fill quickly. Book it before the Friday crossing.
- **Hagerty / ACI / Grundy** must be bound effective the morning of 2026-05-15. Carriers will generally accept VIN-only binding with foreign-title evidence and a 7-10 day window to provide the WA title number — but this must be confirmed by phone at binding, not assumed.

### The single most important fact

**Your Firebird was built in Ohio (Lordstown or Norwood GM Assembly).** That fact is the basis for an HTSUS **9801.00.10** "U.S. Goods Returning" claim, which makes the vehicle **duty-free** at the U.S. border. (Authority: HTSUS heading **9801.00.10**; **19 CFR 10.1**.) Without that documentation, you fall to HTSUS **8703.24.0190** at **2.5%** duty — about **US$900** on a US$36,000 declared value. The Pontiac Historical Services (PHS) build sheet (~US$70 from phs-online.com), VIN-decode evidence of an Ohio plant code, and the cowl-tag photo are the documents that make 9801 stick. Bring them.

## 5. Step-by-Step at Each Agency

### 5.1 At the Alberta registry agent (T-1, day before crossing)

**Address**: Any Alberta registry agent. Your seller likely has one they use; otherwise pick one near the seller's location and confirm it provides notary / Commissioner for Oaths services.

**Bring**: Draft Bill of Sale (English), bank draft for sale price, both parties' photo ID, the vehicle (preferred), the original Alberta Vehicle Registration Certificate.

**What happens**:
1. Seller signs Vehicle Registration Certificate transferring to you (or signs the back over).
2. Both parties sign the Bill of Sale in front of the Commissioner for Oaths or notary.
3. Registry agent stamps and dates the Bill of Sale.
4. You request a **PPR lien search by VIN** ($10). Print the result — keep with the registration.
5. Take photos of: dashboard VIN plate, cowl tag, registration certificate front and back, Bill of Sale signed, PPR printout. Save to your phone and email them to yourself.

**Time**: ~45 minutes if everything is in order.

### 5.2 At CBSA Pacific Highway (T-0 morning)

**Address**: CBSA Pacific Highway commercial / vehicle export office, BC. Truck Crossing — south end of 176 Street, Surrey BC.

**Hours**: Verify by phone the week before. Vehicle export staffing is sometimes M-F only at smaller ports — Pacific Highway typically handles vehicle export during regular business hours, but **call to confirm Friday morning**.

**Bring**: CERS proof-of-report number (printed); original Alberta Vehicle Registration Certificate; signed notarized Bill of Sale (original + 2 copies); PPR lien search printout; photo ID; lender lien-release letter if there ever was a lien; keys.

**What happens**:
1. Pull into the export / commercial lane, not the passenger-traveler lane.
2. Officer asks for CERS proof-of-report number and documents.
3. Officer verifies VIN on vehicle matches paperwork.
4. Officer **stamps the registration** "EXPORTED" or surrenders it (varies by office). You keep a stamped copy.
5. Officer releases the vehicle from Canada.
6. You drive the short distance to the U.S. side.

**Common questions**:
- *"Why are you exporting?"* — "Personal sale; I'm importing for my own use to Washington."
- *"Have you reported under CERS?"* — "Yes, here's the proof-of-report number." Do not show up without it; you will be turned around.

### 5.3 At CBP Blaine (T-0 morning, immediately after CBSA)

**Address**: CBP Blaine port of entry, north end of I-5, Blaine WA.

**Hours**: 24/7 for the truck crossing; vehicle import processing is typically M-F daytime. **Call ahead the week before** to confirm Friday vehicle-import staffing.

**Bring**: All Canadian-side documents (now including CBSA-stamped registration); passport; proof of WA residence (driver's license + utility bill or lease); evidence of US origin (PHS build sheet, VIN-decode printout showing plant code L = Lordstown OH or N = Norwood OH); blank HS-7 and EPA 3520-1 forms (carry your own pre-filled copies and a spare); credit card for MPF; proof of insurance covering the drive home.

**What happens**:
1. Pull into the commercial / import lane (not the regular passenger lane).
2. CBP officer asks: country of origin, value, intent of importation. Be direct: *"I am importing my own vehicle, a 1968 Pontiac Firebird, built in Ohio, US Goods Returning under HTSUS 9801.00.10. Declared value US$36,000. Personal use."*
3. Officer reviews documents; you may be sent to **secondary inspection** for the formal entry. This is normal for vehicles, not a sign of trouble.
4. At secondary, you complete:
   - **HS-7**: check **Box 1** (vehicle ≥25 years old, FMVSS-exempt under 49 CFR 591.5(i)). Sign and date.
   - **EPA 3520-1**: check **Code E** (vehicle ≥21 years and original-equipment configuration, exempt under 40 CFR 85.1511(b)(2)). Sign and date.
   - **Form 7501**: declare HTSUS **9801.00.10** with **8703.24.0190** as fallback. Officer or you fill in classification, value (in USD), country of origin (US), MPF.
5. Present PHS build sheet and VIN-plate photos to support the 9801 claim.
6. Pay MPF (~US$124.70) by credit card.
7. Officer **stamps all three forms** and returns the originals to you. **Do not leave the booth without stamped originals.** Photograph them on the spot.
8. Drive away free into Blaine.

**If 9801 is rejected** (officer's discretion): officer reclassifies under 8703.24.0190; you pay 2.5% × $36,000 = $900 at the booth. The entry then proceeds. You can dispute the classification through the CBP protest process under 19 CFR 174 within 180 days, but this requires a customs attorney/broker and is not a pro-se path. **Consider consulting a customs broker (US$150-300) before the trip if you have any doubt about your 9801 documentation pack** — paying $300 to lock in $900 of duty savings is a reasonable hedge.

### 5.4 At Cascade Auto License, Blaine, WA (T-0 afternoon)

**Address**: Cascade Auto License is the closest WA DOL subagent to the Blaine border crossing. Verify exact address and hours by phone before the trip.

**Bring**: Photo ID; CBP-stamped Form 7501; cash, debit, or credit card.

**What happens**:
1. Buy **WA Trip Permit** ($36, valid 3 days from issuance). The agent prints the permit; you fill in the date carefully (do not pre-fill a future date — DOL instructions specify the dates be filled at issuance).
2. Affix the permit to the vehicle as instructed (usually inside the rear window).

**Why here, not online**: As of 2026, the WA DOL public site does not support self-issue online for unregistered vehicles. The permit must be obtained in person at a DOL office or subagent. Cascade Auto License is the typical pre-stop for cross-border buyers.

### 5.5 At WSP VIN inspection (T+3, scheduled Monday)

**Address**: A Washington State Patrol VIN inspection station. Use the appointment scheduler at https://wsp.wa.gov/driver/schedule-a-vin/ to find the nearest station and book a slot.

**Bring**: Photo ID; CBSA-stamped Alberta registration; CBP-stamped Form 7501; the vehicle on the trip permit; $65 in cash or check (confirm payment methods at booking — some stations do not accept credit cards).

**What happens**:
1. Trooper inspects the vehicle, comparing VIN on the dashboard to VIN on the foreign registration.
2. Looks for signs of VIN tampering or replaced tags.
3. Issues a **VIN inspection certificate** valid 180 days (WAC 308-56A-150).

**Time**: ~30 minutes (most slots are 30-min appointments).

**If the VIN does not match** the foreign registration: the trooper will not issue a certificate. This is a high-risk failure mode — see Section 12 on recovery.

### 5.6 At Seattle Auto Licensing — Quick Title (T+5 to T+7)

**Address**: A King County DOL subagent in Seattle that processes **Quick Title** under RCW 46.12.555. Confirm by phone that they handle foreign-title imports for Quick Title (some subagents prefer to forward foreign titles to the central DOL office, which adds 6-10 weeks).

**Bring**:
1. CBP-stamped HS-7 (original)
2. CBP-stamped EPA 3520-1 (original)
3. CBP-stamped Form 7501 (original)
4. CBSA-stamped Alberta Vehicle Registration Certificate (original)
5. Notarized Bill of Sale (original, English)
6. WSP VIN inspection certificate (original, dated within 180 days)
7. Vehicle Title Application TD-420-001 (filled out)
8. Proof of WA insurance (Hagerty/ACI declarations page, dated 2026-05-15 effective)
9. Photo ID + proof of WA residency (utility bill, lease, or driver's license matching the address)
10. Payment for ~US$3,990 (credit card, debit, or cashier's check; verify acceptable methods at appointment booking)

**What happens**:
1. Clerk reviews all documents in order.
2. Clerk calculates use tax: greater of declared price or FMV × 10.25%. Clerk may pull Hagerty Valuation Tool / NADA Classic to check FMV. If your declared price ($36,000) is at or above NADA "good condition" value, use tax is $3,690.
3. Clerk processes title application and registration. Pays use tax + RTA + title fees + plate fee + Quick Title $50 surcharge.
4. Clerk **issues title certificate same day** under RCW 46.12.555 (this is the Quick Title benefit — without it, the title is mailed in 6-10 weeks).
5. Clerk issues plates same day.
6. You leave with: title certificate, plate(s), receipts.
7. Email the WA title number to your insurer.

**Time**: ~45-90 minutes including wait time.

## 6. Key Decisions You Need to Make

These are decision points where there is no single right answer — only trade-offs. Each is framed so you can decide based on your own facts.

### 6.1 HTSUS 9801.00.10 with documentation pack vs. paying ~$900 duty under 8703.24.0190

| Path | Cost at the booth | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Bring full 9801 documentation pack** (PHS build sheet, VIN-decode showing Ohio plant code, cowl tag photo) | $0 duty + $124.70 MPF | Officer may still reject — discretionary call. Doc pack costs ~US$70 + your time. |
| **Pay 8703.24 duty without claiming 9801** | $900 duty + $124.70 MPF | None at the booth — entry is straightforward. |
| **Hire a customs broker to pre-file** | $150-300 broker fee + duty (or $0 if 9801 accepted) | Pro: broker locks in classification before you arrive. Con: cost. |

**Authority**: HTSUS 9801.00.10; 19 CFR 10.1 (documentation for US Goods Returning); CBP Informed Compliance Publication: *Classification of Motor Vehicles*.

**Engine origin in this case**: The vehicle has a period-correct Pontiac 400 V8 from an early-1970s donor, replacing the original block. This is **not** an LS swap or modernization. The 400 V8 was a factory engine option for the 1968 Firebird 400, and the replacement engine was built at GM's Pontiac engine plant in Pontiac, Michigan — so the engine is also US-origin. A like-vintage same-family replacement is generally treated as **maintenance / repair**, not "improvement abroad" under 19 CFR 10.1, and the US-origin engine reinforces (does not undermine) the 9801.00.10 claim. Disclosure language to a CBP officer if asked: *"Period-correct Pontiac 400 V8, US-built, replacing the original block."*

**Consider consulting a customs broker** if any of the following are true: the cowl tag is missing or unreadable; the engine has been swapped to a **non-period-correct** configuration (e.g., LS swap, modern fuel injection retrofit, non-Pontiac engine); the seller's chain of title shows the vehicle has spent time in a non-North-American country; the PHS report is delayed beyond the trip date. A period-correct same-family swap (your situation) is generally not a broker trigger, but a broker is still optional insurance against an unusually strict officer.

| Path | Cost | Restrictions | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard registration** | ~$60-100/year recurring | None on use | Daily driver, 5,000+ miles/year |
| **Collector plate** (RCW 46.18.220) | $35 **once**, no annual renewal | Use limited to "public displays, parades, club activities, exhibitions, tours, and similar uses" plus "incidental other use" | Weekend / show car, ~1,000 miles/year |

**Authority**: RCW 46.18.220 (Collector Vehicle plates); RCW 46.16A.220 (Year-of-manufacture plates).

**Watch out**: WSP enforcement of the collector-use restriction is sporadic but real. Daily-commuting on collector plates can result in citations and forced switch to standard registration. If your honest answer is "I'll drive this once a month for shows and weekends," collector pays for itself in 1-2 years. If your honest answer is "this might become my daily-driver," choose standard.

### 6.3 Quick Title vs. Standard Title

| Path | Cost | Timing | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quick Title** (RCW 46.12.555) | +$50 surcharge | Title certificate same-day | Buyers who need title quickly to update insurance, sell, or finance |
| **Standard Title** | No surcharge | Title certificate mailed 6-10 weeks | Buyers who don't need the certificate immediately |

**Both produce identical legal title.** The only difference is when the paper certificate is in your hand. Insurance carriers (Hagerty/ACI/Grundy) generally need the WA title number within 7-10 days of binding — Quick Title meets that window comfortably; Standard Title misses it and may require the carrier to extend the binder. Consider Quick Title the default unless cash flow is tight.

### 6.4 Hagerty vs. ACI vs. Grundy as binding carrier

Per the insurance team's analysis (sources: `firebird-hagerty-prefill.md`, `firebird-american-collectors-prefill.md`, `firebird-grundy-prefill.md`):

| Carrier | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Hagerty** | Primary, but disclose engine | Established VIN-bind path with foreign-title evidence; Hagerty Drivers Club Plus includes flatbed-to-shop-of-choice; Hagerty Valuation Tool supports the $36,000 agreed value directly. **Caveat**: Hagerty's underwriting appetite skews toward original / numbers-matching cars. Disclose the period-correct 400 V8 swap up-front; ask whether agreed value adjusts and whether a separate appraiser is required. |
| **American Collectors (ACI)** | Secondary, may price better | Similar VIN-bind capability; smaller carrier, useful for premium benchmark; **appetite extends to driver-grade and lightly modified** — a non-numbers-matching but period-correct drivetrain is well within their appetite, often without an appraisal surcharge. |
| **Grundy** | Benchmark only | Historically strong on unlimited mileage but VIN-bind on foreign title needs explicit phone confirmation; appetite for non-numbers-matching also requires phone confirmation. |

**All three** should be called for quotes. Bind with whichever (a) confirms VIN-only binding with foreign title, (b) provides flatbed-to-shop-of-choice towing, (c) accepts the Hagerty Valuation Tool + PHS build sheet as agreed-value evidence at $36,000, and (d) offers the lowest total cost of ownership across the policy term.

**Coverage selections** (consistent across carriers):
- Liability BI 100/300, PD $100,000
- UM/UIM matching limits
- Collision and Comprehensive deductibles $500
- MedPay $5,000
- Spare-parts coverage as included
- Agreed value $36,000

**Consider consulting a licensed insurance agent** before binding if: you have any coverage gap with your existing daily-driver policy (e.g., umbrella that needs to extend); you have a teenage driver in the household; the vehicle's intended use is unclear (collector vs. daily); or your declared value is contested.

**Note on the period-correct engine swap**: All three carriers will accept the configuration as long as it's disclosed at quote time. Failing to disclose and then having a claim where the engine is part of the loss can result in coverage disputes (misrepresentation defenses). The prefill packets include the engine fact in the "Key facts to volunteer immediately" section — read it verbatim at the start of every call.

### 6.5 Timeline Pattern — In-Country Sale (default) vs. Pay-from-US / File-from-US (alternative)

**Important clarification on the 72-hour rule.** The 72 hours runs between **submitting the CERS declaration** and **physically driving through the CBSA export booth**. It is NOT a wait between the CBSA booth and the CBP booth — those happen minutes apart on the same day, at the same crossing. (Authority: CBSA Memorandum **D-20-1-1**.)

Because filing is decoupled from physical possession, two patterns are legally valid.

#### Pattern A — In-Country Sale, then File (the default in Section 2)

You travel to Alberta, complete the sale at a registry agent on T-4, file CERS the same afternoon with the documents in hand, then cross at T-0. Time in Canada: ~5-7 days. Lower counterparty risk because you inspect before paying.

#### Pattern B — Pay-from-US, File-from-US, then Travel

```
T-10        : Register CERS account (1-3 business days for non-Canadian
              individuals; alternative: have the seller file or use a
              customs broker)
T-8 to T-5  : Lock the deal with seller — signed agreement, photos of
              registration / VIN plate / cowl tag, wire transfer or escrow
T-4 morning : File CERS from the US with VIN, exporter/consignee details,
              port of exit, planned crossing date, declared value in CAD
T-3, T-2    : 72-hour clock running + travel preparation
T-1         : Travel to Alberta
T-0 morning : At Alberta registry agent — notarized Bill of Sale,
              signed-over Vehicle Registration Certificate, fresh PPR
              lien search printout
T-0 same day: Drive to Pacific Highway, cross CBSA -> CBP
```

CERS only requires **information** (VIN, parties, port, date, value) — not physical possession of the vehicle or the registration certificate. (See Section 3 for what the CBSA booth requires; that does require physical documents but not until T-0.) So you can pay from the US, file from the US, and travel to Alberta only 1-2 days before crossing.

#### Trade-offs

| Factor | Pattern A | Pattern B |
|---|---|---|
| Time in Canada | 5-7 days | 1-2 days |
| Pricing risk | Locked at sale day | Locked early via wire |
| Counterparty risk | Low (inspect before paying) | Higher (wire to stranger before inspection) |
| Buffer if something goes wrong | 4 days between sale and crossing | Day-of compressed |
| CERS account hassle | Optional if seller files | Buyer almost certainly files |
| Best for | First-time imports, unknown sellers | Repeat buyers, well-documented sellers, escrow-mediated transactions |

#### Pattern B Risk Mitigations

If you choose Pattern B, mitigate the wire-to-stranger risk:

- **Live video walk-around** of the vehicle showing VIN plate and cowl tag, dated by the seller
- **Photo of seller's government ID** matching the name on the Vehicle Registration Certificate
- **Reverse phone lookup** to verify the seller's phone matches a public Alberta address
- **Deposit-then-balance** structure (e.g., 20-30% deposit on signed agreement, balance by bank draft delivered at the Alberta registry agent on T-0)
- **Escrow service** for vehicles above US$25,000 (escrow.com vehicle fee ~1% of value)
- **Alberta-side proxy** — if you have a friend, family member, or paid inspector in Alberta, have them do the physical inspection before payment
- **Verify the registration certificate** is genuine by calling the seller's local Service Alberta registry to confirm format and issue date

#### Recommendation

**Stay with Pattern A unless you have a specific reason to compress.** The 4-day buffer between sale and crossing absorbs the inevitable hiccups (lien surfacing in PPR, name-mismatch on registration, registry agent unavailable, engine configuration not as photographed). Pattern B is legitimate and works for buyers with established seller relationships, escrow-mediated deals, or trusted Alberta-side inspectors. For a Kijiji-style first transaction with a private seller, Pattern A is the conservative choice.

The pro-se filing-guide treats Pattern A as the primary timeline (Section 2). If you elect Pattern B, the deadlines remain the same — only the location of the buyer at each step changes.

### 6.6 PIP rejection in writing vs. accept WA default PIP

Washington allows you to **reject Personal Injury Protection (PIP) in writing** at policy inception, or take the default $10,000 PIP that the carrier will offer.

| Path | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| **Reject PIP in writing** | Lower premium; you rely on health insurance + MedPay for medical bills after a covered crash. Standard practice for collector policies on a single, well-insured driver who already has comprehensive health insurance. |
| **Accept default PIP ($10,000)** | Higher premium; PIP pays medical and lost-wage benefits regardless of fault, on a no-fault basis. |

**Most classic-collector buyers reject PIP in writing.** This requires a signed rejection form retained by the carrier — verbally telling the agent "no thanks" is not sufficient. **Consider consulting your insurance agent** about whether your existing health and disability insurance fully replaces the PIP benefit.

## 7. Warnings — Common Mistakes

The most common ways this goes wrong, drawn from the source bundles:

1. **Showing up at the wrong CBSA port.** Not every CBSA port handles vehicle export. Pacific Highway does, but vehicle-export staffing is sometimes M-F only. **Call the port the week before** to confirm hours and that vehicle export is handled the day you plan to cross.

2. **Filing CERS less than 72 hours before crossing.** This is mandatory, not advisory. The clock runs from CBSA receipt time, not your submission time. **Submit by noon Monday for a Friday morning crossing.** Showing up with a 30-hour-old report gets you turned around.

3. **Driving to CBP before CBSA releases the vehicle.** A driver who skips the Canadian export booth and goes straight to the U.S. side will be sent back. **CBSA first, then CBP.** Always.

4. **Showing up with just "1968" — no build date.** The 25-year FMVSS exemption is calculated by build month/year, not model year. CBP wants to see the build plate. Carry photos of the cowl tag and the PHS report.

5. **Underdeclaring purchase price for use tax.** Buyer declares $5,000 on a Bill of Sale to reduce WA use tax on a car worth $36,000. The DOL clerk pulls Hagerty/NADA, calculates use tax on FMV anyway, and now your Bill of Sale is on file showing tax fraud. **Declare the actual purchase price.** If you bought below market, prepare to pay use tax on FMV and don't fight it — this is RCW 82.12.010(7).

6. **Forgetting state use tax in the project budget.** Federal-side math is small ($125 in MPF). State-side math is the dominant cost (~$3,990). If you budgeted "$500 to import a car," you'll be short by an order of magnitude.

7. **Trip permit issued after crossing the border.** WA's 3-day permit must be obtained **before** driving on WA roads. Buy it at Cascade Auto License (Blaine) immediately after CBP, before you leave for Seattle. **Driving without it is unlawful** even if "everyone does it."

8. **VIN inspection certificate expires before DOL appointment.** WSP certificate valid 180 days under WAC 308-56A-150 — comfortable margin. But if you skip a DOL appointment and let the trip permit expire, you may need to redo logistics (second trip permit, re-schedule WSP slot).

9. **Insurance bound on a VIN that turns out wrong at WSP inspection.** Photograph the dash VIN before binding. If the VIN on the dash differs from the Bill of Sale, resolve with the seller before the export, not after.

10. **Forgetting to insist on stamped originals at CBP.** Without CBP-stamped HS-7 and EPA 3520-1 originals, no Washington office will issue a title. Replacements through the port of entry can take weeks. **Insist on stamped originals before leaving the booth, and photograph them on the spot.**

## 8. When to Get a Lawyer or Customs Broker

This is administrative procedure, not litigation. Most importers complete this process without an attorney. The thresholds below identify situations where the cost of professional help is small relative to the consequences of getting it wrong.

### Strongly consider a customs broker (US$150-300) BEFORE crossing if any apply

- The cowl tag is missing or unreadable, weakening your 9801 documentation pack.
- The engine has been swapped or modified (LS swap, modern fuel injection retrofit) — creates a fact question under EPA 40 CFR 85.1511(b)(2) "original equipment configuration."
- The seller's chain of title shows the vehicle was in a non-North-American country (e.g., Europe) at any point.
- The PHS build sheet has not arrived by T-3 and cannot be re-ordered in time.
- The total declared value exceeds US$50,000 (broker error costs scale with declared value).

A broker can pre-file the entry electronically through ABI, lock in classification before you arrive, and represent you at the booth if there's a dispute. **Cost-vs-benefit: $150-300 broker fee is small relative to ~$900 of avoided duty plus the time cost of a turned-around entry.**

### Strongly consider a customs / international trade attorney if any apply

- **CBP rejects 9801 at the booth and the duty owed exceeds US$3,000** (i.e., declared value over ~US$120,000). A formal protest under 19 CFR 174 has a 180-day window and is not a pro se path.
- **The seller's title chain has gaps** (estate sale, divorce settlement, judgment lien) that cannot be cleared with a Bill of Sale and lien-release letter.
- **An active lien shows up on the PPR search** and the seller cannot produce a release.
- **WA DOL refuses to title** the vehicle on the foreign registration after the VIN inspection passes (rare — usually a documentation issue resolvable by re-presenting; if persistent, indicates a deeper issue).
- **A lien dispute arises** (a Canadian secured party claims rights in the vehicle after you have it in Washington).

For these scenarios, the typical lawyer fee is US$500-2,000 for a focused consultation, vs. the loss of a US$36,000 vehicle if the dispute goes wrong. **Consider consulting a Washington vehicle-titling attorney** (look for one with experience in cross-border imports — small bar, often listed by the WA State Bar referral service at wsba.org).

### Insurance — consider consulting a licensed insurance agent if

- You have an umbrella policy (it must be matched in BI/UM-UIM limits to the underlying classic policy).
- You have a teenage driver in the household (rateability changes significantly).
- You have any disability or chronic-condition issue that affects PIP analysis.
- You need transit-binder coverage and the carrier won't VIN-bind.

### Beyond pro se — escalation triggers

If, at any point in this process, any of the following occur, **stop and consult a licensed attorney before continuing**:

- Criminal charge or potential criminal liability (e.g., title fraud allegation).
- Risk of permanent loss of the vehicle (seizure, forfeiture).
- Multi-state jurisdictional dispute (e.g., a claimant in another state asserts ownership).
- Confusion or significant disagreement between agencies about your status.

## 11. What Could Go Wrong & How to Recover

### 11.1 Turned around at CBSA (export side)

**Causes**: CERS not filed or filed less than 72 hours before; PPR shows a lien; documents incomplete; chosen port not staffed for vehicle export that day.

**Recovery**:
1. Do not argue with the officer. Drive back into Canada (you have not yet exported).
2. Identify the missing piece. If CERS late: re-file CERS with a new crossing date, wait 72 hours, return.
3. If a lien is found: contact the prior lender for a release letter. Lien clearance may take days.
4. If the port is not staffed: drive to the next-nearest staffed port (Aldergrove/Lynden or Sumas/Sumas) and verify by phone before driving.
5. Re-bind insurance for the new effective date.

### 11.2 Turned around at CBP (import side)

**Causes**: CBSA didn't release; HS-7 / EPA 3520-1 not properly filled; 9801 documentation insufficient; declared value disputed; secondary inspection finds VIN discrepancy.

**Recovery**:
1. Do **not** drive away. CBP turning you around is more serious than CBSA — you are between countries.
2. Listen carefully to the officer's stated reason. Ask: "What documentation would resolve this issue?"
3. If documentation is fixable on the spot (e.g., wrong HS-7 box checked): correct and re-submit.
4. If 9801 is rejected: pay the 2.5% duty and proceed. The entry is final; protest only via 19 CFR 174 if you elect to challenge.
5. If VIN discrepancy: you may be allowed to return to Canada to resolve with the seller. **Document everything** — photos, names of officers, time of contact. **Consider consulting a customs broker before re-attempting.**
6. If the entry is denied entirely: the vehicle returns to Canada. You'll need to reset the timeline (CBSA re-export, etc.) which costs at least one full week.

### 11.3 Missed CERS 72-hour window

**Causes**: User submitted CERS Tuesday for a Friday crossing (less than 72 hours).

**Recovery**:
1. **Reschedule the crossing** to the following week.
2. Re-bind insurance for the new effective date.
3. Re-schedule WSP and DOL appointments.
4. Notify Alberta seller of the delay; secure the vehicle (storage, key handling) for the extra days.
5. **Lesson**: always submit CERS by **noon Monday** for a Friday crossing.

### 11.4 WSP VIN inspection appointment unavailable

**Causes**: 2-week scheduler was full when you booked at T-7; appointment cancelled by WSP.

**Recovery**:
1. Book the next available slot at any WSP station within driving distance of your trip permit.
2. **If the next slot is past the 3-day trip permit window**: buy a second permit ($36, RCW 46.16A.320) at any DOL subagent.
3. If next slot is past 6 days: buy a third permit. WA caps at 3 permits per vehicle per year — past that, the vehicle becomes legally undriveable until titled.
4. Alternative: some DOL-authorized inspection sites also do VIN inspections — confirm at https://dol.wa.gov inspection-site list.

### 11.5 Trip permit expired without registration done

**Causes**: VIN inspection delayed; Quick Title appointment slipped; user lost track of dates.

**Recovery**:
1. **Stop driving the vehicle immediately.** Park it at home or storage.
2. Buy a second / third trip permit at any DOL subagent ($36 each).
3. Reschedule any missed appointment.
4. Resume driving only after the new permit is in hand and dated.
5. WSP citations for driving without registration / without trip permit run $200-500+ and can result in vehicle impoundment.

### 11.6 9801.00.10 rejected at the booth

**Causes**: Officer judged documentation insufficient; cowl tag unreadable; PHS not ordered.

**Recovery at the booth**:
1. Pay 2.5% duty (~US$900 on $36,000) by credit card.
2. Get stamped Form 7501 reflecting 8703.24.0190 classification.
3. Proceed with import.

**Recovery after the fact** (only if savings justify the work):
1. File a CBP **Protest under 19 CFR 174** within 180 days of liquidation.
2. This requires legal/broker assistance — protest petitions for personal vehicle imports rarely succeed without strong post-hoc documentation (PHS report obtained late, marque expert affidavit).
3. **Cost-vs-benefit**: $500-2,000 of broker/attorney time to recover $900 of duty is rarely worthwhile. Most users accept the duty and move on.

### 11.7 WA DOL refuses to title on foreign documents

**Causes**: Documentation incomplete; CBP-stamped originals missing; clerk unfamiliar with foreign-title imports.

**Recovery**:
1. Ask the clerk for the **specific reason** in writing (most subagents will tell you verbally; if not, ask for the supervisor).
2. If a stamped form is missing: contact the CBP port of entry to request a duplicate / certified copy. Allow 2-6 weeks. Buy additional trip permits in the interim.
3. If the clerk is simply unfamiliar with foreign-title imports: ask the supervisor to escalate to **DOL Olympia headquarters** (the central title office handles foreign-title imports routinely). Some Seattle-area subagents will refer foreign-title applications to Olympia, which adds 6-10 weeks but completes successfully.
4. If persistent: **consider consulting a WA vehicle-titling attorney** — the typical fee is $200-500 for a focused phone consultation.
