# Seller Instructions — Alberta Private Sale for US Export

A plain-language packet to hand the Alberta seller. The seller does not need to know US import law — only what the buyer needs from them and why. Use these sections as written; edit only the bracketed placeholders.

> **Important: Alberta does not issue "titles" the way US states do.** The buyer (and US officials) may use the word "title" — what they actually need is the **Vehicle Registration Certificate** plus a properly drafted **Bill of Sale**, plus a **lien-free** confirmation. The packet below uses the terms the seller will recognize.

---

## What the Seller Will Be Asked For

A plain-English list to share verbatim:

> Hi [Seller name],
>
> I'm buying your [year/make/model, VIN: ____] and exporting it to the United States. I'd like to make this easy for both of us. Below is everything I'll need from you. Most of it you already have — the rest is quick to obtain.
>
> ### Documents you give me at the time of sale
>
> 1. **Original Vehicle Registration Certificate** (the paper Service Alberta issues — what you would normally hand a new buyer in Alberta). I need the **original**, not a photocopy. You can transfer it into my name at a registry, or sign the back over to me — your choice. **US Customs treats this as the equivalent of a "title" even though Alberta doesn't use that word.**
>
> 2. **Bill of Sale** — I'll bring a draft to the sale. It will state the VIN, full sale price (in Canadian dollars), date of sale, your full name and address, my full name and US address, the odometer reading, both signatures, and the words "**sold as-is for export to the United States**." I'd like both of us to sign it in front of a **Commissioner for Oaths or Notary Public** (any Alberta registry agent can do this for $20–60). US Customs has been known to ask for a notarized Bill of Sale, and not having one can mean a long wait or a rejected entry. Please plan to sign it at a registry agent on the day of the sale.
>
> 3. **Two pieces of identification** — government photo ID (driver's licence is fine). The notary needs to see your ID to swear the Bill of Sale.
>
> ### Documents you'll help me obtain (mostly easy and online)
>
> 4. **Lien search from the Alberta Personal Property Registry (PPR)** — confirms there is no loan or lien recorded against the vehicle. I can run this search myself for about $10 at any registry agent or online — but I'll need the **VIN exactly as registered**, which means I might ask you to send me a photo of the registration certificate before I drive up. Please don't be offended by this — it's standard practice and protects both of us.
>
> 5. **If there ever was a loan against the vehicle**: a **lien release letter** from the lender confirming the loan is paid off. If you took out a loan years ago and paid it off, sometimes the lender forgets to remove the entry — the PPR search will catch this. If anything turns up, I'll work with you to clear it before the sale.
>
> ### Things that help me at the US border (please send before I drive up)
>
> 6. **Photos of**:
>    - The **VIN plate** on the dashboard (visible through the windshield on the driver's side)
>    - The **cowl tag** (a small metal plate on the firewall under the hood) — for a 1968 Firebird this tag has the build date and the assembly plant code. I need this for US Customs.
>    - The **registration certificate** (front and back)
>    - **Any service records or paperwork** you have for the car (build sheets, factory documents, dealer paperwork). For a 1968 Pontiac, original paperwork has real value to me.
>
> 7. **Build/manufacturing details** if you know them. The car was built at one of the GM plants in **Ohio** (Lordstown or Norwood). If you have any factory paperwork showing this, please bring it. **This documentation is what saves me 2.5% in US import duty — about [USD amount] on this purchase.** It's the most valuable paperwork in the deal.
>
> ### What I'm NOT asking you to do
>
> - You do not need to deal with US Customs or US paperwork. That's all on me.
> - You do not need to deliver the car to the border. I will drive it.
> - You do not need to charge me Alberta sales tax — Alberta doesn't have one for private sales between individuals.
> - You do not need to give me your bank info. Payment will be by [bank draft / certified cheque / wire — buyer specifies].
>
> ### Day-of-sale checklist
>
> | What | Where |
> |---|---|
> | Sign Bill of Sale (notarized) | Alberta registry agent |
> | Sign Vehicle Registration Certificate over to me | Same registry, or done privately |
> | Hand over original registration | Same |
> | Pull PPR lien search printout | Same registry, ~$10 |
> | Hand over keys, owner's manual, service records, photos | Wherever the car is |
> | Take photos of VIN plate and cowl tag | Wherever the car is |
>
> Plan on about **45 minutes at the registry agent** to get all of this done. I'll bring the draft Bill of Sale and the cheque/draft.
>
> Thanks again for working with me on this. The car is going to a good home.
>
> [Buyer name, US contact info]

## Buyer's Backstop Checklist (Internal — Don't Send to Seller)

Things the buyer should verify independently, even if the seller is helpful:

- [ ] Run the **PPR lien search** yourself before driving north (alberta.ca/personal-property-registry, ~CAD 10). Don't rely solely on the seller's printout.
- [ ] Run a **Carfax Canada / CarProof** report (~CAD 50). Catches accident, odometer, and title-brand issues.
- [ ] Order a **Pontiac Historical Services (PHS) report** (~USD 70 at phs-online.com). Confirms factory build, original engine, dealer destination, and assembly plant. Independent evidence of US origin for HTSUS 9801.00.10. **For a 1968 Firebird, this is the single most valuable pre-purchase document.**
- [ ] Confirm the seller's **government ID matches** the name on the registration. Mismatched names (estate sale, divorce settlement, name change) need extra paperwork.
- [ ] Confirm the **VIN on the registration matches the VIN on the car** (dash, cowl tag, frame stamp if accessible). Old vehicles sometimes have transcription errors or replaced tags.
- [ ] Confirm the **odometer reading on the Bill of Sale matches** the dashboard. Photograph the dashboard at sale.
- [ ] Pay by traceable instrument (bank draft, certified cheque, or wire). Avoid cash above CAD 10,000 (triggers FINTRAC reporting and looks suspicious to CBSA).

## What "Clear Canadian Title" Means in Alberta Terms

If the seller is confused by "clear Canadian title," the buyer should clarify:

> "I'm using 'title' the way Americans do. In Alberta, I think the equivalent is the **Vehicle Registration Certificate** plus a **lien-free PPR search**. I just need to know that nobody else has a financial claim on the car and that you can legally sell it to me. The PPR search costs about $10 and any registry agent can pull it in five minutes."

This usually resolves the confusion. Some sellers also call the registration certificate the "ownership" — common Canadian usage but not consistent province-to-province.

## Province Quick-Reference for Future Use

| Province | Ownership Document | Lien Registry | Provincial Sales Tax (private sale) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alberta** | Vehicle Registration Certificate | PPR | None |
| British Columbia | Vehicle Registration / Transfer/Tax Form (APV9T) | BC Online PPR | PST 12% (paid by buyer at registration; not an issue if exported directly) |
| Ontario | Vehicle Permit ("ownership") + UVIP | Ontario PPR | RST 13% (similar; export reduces) |
| Quebec | Certificat d'immatriculation | RDPRM | QST 9.975% |
| Saskatchewan | Registration | SK PPR | PST 6% |

For Alberta specifically: **no provincial sales tax obstacle, no separate "title" document, lien check via PPR.**
