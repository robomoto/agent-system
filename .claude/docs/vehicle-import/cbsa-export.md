# CBSA Export — Used Self-Propelled Vehicles

Canadian export procedure for a privately-owned used vehicle leaving Canada permanently. Authority: Customs Act and **CBSA Memorandum D-20-1-1** (Export Reporting). Effective system: **CERS** (Canadian Export Reporting System), which replaced the legacy paper B13A and the prior Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system.

## The 72-Hour Rule

Used self-propelled vehicles must be reported to CBSA **at least 72 hours before** their export, not at the booth. This is a longstanding requirement regardless of value. Reporting platform:

- **CERS portal** — submitted electronically, generates a proof-of-report number that the exporter presents at the port of exit
- The 72-hour clock starts when CBSA receives the declaration, not when the user submits it (small allowance for processing — submit slightly earlier in practice)

The rule does **not** apply to:
- New vehicles being exported by a manufacturer/dealer with separate processes
- Personal effects under temporary import/export

## Required Document Set (Export Side)

CBSA expects the exporter to present, at minimum:

| Document | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vehicle title or provincial registration** (original, in seller's name OR transferred to buyer) | Provincial registry | Most provinces issue a Certificate of Registration, not a "title" in the US sense. The original registration plus a Bill of Sale documents ownership. |
| **Bill of Sale** | Buyer + seller | Should include VIN, sale price (CAD), date, full names/addresses of both parties, signatures. **Notarization recommended** — some CBP officers want it on the US side. |
| **Lien release** (if any lien previously recorded) | Lender / provincial PPR search printout | Vehicle cannot be exported with an active lien. PPR search demonstrates clear title. |
| **Photo ID** | Driver's license / passport | Both buyer and seller IDs at sale; only buyer's needed at border. |
| **CERS proof-of-report** | CERS portal printout / email | Demonstrates 72-hour rule satisfied. |
| **Recall clearance** (if requested) | Manufacturer | Rarely requested for vehicles >25 years old, but a manufacturer "clear of recalls" letter is sometimes asked for. |

## Designated Export Offices

Not every CBSA port handles vehicle export. CERS submission specifies the intended port of exit; the user must cross at that port. Major designated offices for AB/BC → WA/MT/ID:

- **Coutts, AB / Sweetgrass, MT** — high-volume crossing; vehicle export handled
- **Pacific Highway, BC / Blaine, WA (Truck Crossing)** — preferred for commercial-style vehicle moves
- **Aldergrove, BC / Lynden, WA**
- **Sumas, BC / Sumas, WA**
- **Osoyoos, BC / Oroville, WA**

**Always verify** the chosen port handles vehicle export at the time of travel — call the port's CBSA office. Hours vary; some ports do not handle vehicle export on weekends.

## Provincial Considerations

Each Canadian province has its own export wrinkles. See `alberta-washington-route.md` for AB-specific details (PPR lien search, no PST, registration-vs-title quirk).

## Costs (Export Side)

Generally **no CBSA fees** for personal-use vehicle export. Costs that may apply:

- Provincial lien search (CAD ~$10-50)
- Notary for Bill of Sale (CAD ~$20-100)
- GST: Private sales between individuals are generally not GST-able. Dealer sales to non-residents for export may be zero-rated or eligible for rebate via CRA Form GST189 — confirm with seller and CRA.
- Optional: customs broker (CAD ~$100-300); not required for personal vehicle export.

## Timing — Export Day

1. **T-72h+**: Submit CERS declaration, receive proof-of-report number
2. **T-day**: Arrive at designated CBSA port **before** crossing into the US
3. **CBSA booth**: Present documents, surrender or stamp registration as exported, receive export stamp/release
4. **Drive across to CBP booth** on the US side and proceed to import (see `cbp-import.md`)

If the user goes to CBP first without CBSA-stamping the export, CBP will turn them around. CBSA must release the vehicle from Canada first.
