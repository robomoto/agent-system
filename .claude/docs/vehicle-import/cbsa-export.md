# CBSA Export — Used Self-Propelled Vehicles

Canadian export procedure for a privately-owned used vehicle leaving Canada permanently. Authority: *Customs Act* (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (2nd Supp.)), s. 95, and **CBSA Memorandum D20-1-1** (Export Reporting, ISSN 2369-2391, issued October 24, 2024, modified 2024-10-21). The current memorandum is at: https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/dm-md/d20/d20-1-1-eng.html

## Destination-Specific Rule for Permanently Exported Conveyances (¶ 20)

The October 2024 revision of D20-1-1 placed permanently exported conveyances — including vehicles, motorcycles, ATVs, boats, and off-road equipment — under a **destination-specific** rule in paragraph 20. The operative text:

> Permanently exported conveyances include but are not limited to: vehicles, motorcycles, all-terrain vehicles, boats, airplanes, train engines and off-road equipment. All conveyances must be identified using the conveyance identification number (e.g. vehicle identification number, hull identification number of a boat or any other unique serial number). Depending on the final destination of the goods, exporters must follow the reporting instructions mentioned below:
> - When a conveyance is permanently exported to the **United States**: Documentation identifying the conveyance identification number must be presented to the CBSA **upon request**
> - When a conveyance is permanently exported to **countries other than the United States**: An export declaration quoting the conveyance identification number in the proper field must be presented to the CBSA within the prescribed timeframes (refer to the "Timeframes" section) using the applicable reporting method (refer to the "Reporting methods" section)

**What this means in practice:**

| Destination | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **United States** | Have documentation identifying the VIN available; present it on request at the booth. No CERS declaration filed. |
| **Any other country** | File a CERS export declaration with the standard timeframes (typically 72 hours before export for used self-propelled vehicles). |

> **Historical note.** Prior versions of D20-1-1 treated used self-propelled vehicles as a special category requiring CERS reporting regardless of destination — a "vehicle carve-out" that forced the 72-hour rule for all exports. The October 2024 revision superseded that approach by moving permanently exported conveyances into the destination-specific rule under ¶ 20. Earlier versions of this doc bundle reflected the old rule; the current rule, verified at the URL above, is ¶ 20. The 72-hour CERS framing should be treated as stale for US-bound vehicle exports.

## Path A — US-Bound Vehicle Export (Documentation-on-Request)

For an AB → WA or any Canada → US vehicle export:

- **No CERS export declaration is filed.**
- The exporter must have documentation identifying the VIN available for presentation at the CBSA export booth.
- Documents to have available (present on request):
  - Original Vehicle Registration Certificate (provincial, signed over to the buyer)
  - Notarized Bill of Sale
  - PPR / provincial lien search printout
  - Photo ID
- The CBSA officer may ask for these, or may wave the vehicle through. Both outcomes are normal under ¶ 20.
- Carry a printed copy of the D20-1-1 ¶ 20 excerpt (binder reference `12-cbsa-d20-1-1-paragraph-20.pdf`) in case an officer questions the no-CERS approach. The verbatim rule is the most direct response to a booth challenge.

**CERS accounts** remain available and functional for non-US exports. A CERS account created for this project is not wasted — it would be needed for any future export to a non-US destination.

## Path B — Non-US-Bound Vehicle Export (CERS Declaration Required)

For vehicle exports to countries other than the United States, the pre-October-2024 procedure still applies. The CERS declaration is required, with the standard timeframes under D20-1-1.

### 72-hour rule (non-US destinations)

Used self-propelled vehicles must be **reported** to CBSA **at least 72 hours before** their physical export.

**Critical clarification — what the 72 hours measures:**

> The 72-hour clock runs between **submitting the CERS declaration** and **physically driving the vehicle through the CBSA export booth**. It is NOT a wait between the CBSA booth and the CBP booth — those happen minutes apart on the same day, across the same border crossing.

The vehicle does not sit on the Canadian side for three days. CBSA simply needs your declaration on file for 72 hours so they can review it before you arrive at the booth.

Reporting platform:

- **CERS portal** — submitted electronically, generates a proof-of-report number that the exporter presents at the port of exit
- The clock starts when CBSA **receives** the declaration, not when the user submits it (submit slightly earlier in practice)

### Weekends and the 72-hour clock (non-US destinations)

The 72-hour rule is **wall-clock**: Saturdays and Sundays count. A Friday-morning submission can technically support a Monday-morning crossing.

In practice, two complications make weekend-spanning submissions tighter than the math suggests:

1. **CBSA review/acceptance staffing is light over weekends.** The CERS portal accepts submissions 24/7, but a declaration submitted Friday afternoon may not move from "Pending" to "Accepted" until Monday morning.
2. **Submission timestamp matters precisely.** Friday 9 AM submission → Monday 9 AM crossing = exactly 72 hours, meets the rule. Friday 5 PM submission → Monday 9 AM crossing = ~64 hours, **does not meet**.

Practical guidance: submit ≥96 hours before the planned crossing, and avoid weekend-spanning submissions when possible.

### CERS Filing Prerequisites (non-US destinations)

#### 1. Account access

- A **CERS account** at the CBSA Client Portal. For a non-Canadian individual, registering takes ~1-3 business days.
- Alternatives if registering is impractical:
  - The **seller** files (may already have a Business Number / CRA account)
  - A **customs broker / service provider** files on your behalf (~CAD 100-300)

#### 2. Information about the export

- VIN (full 17-digit, or 13-digit for pre-1981 vehicles)
- Year, make, model, colour, odometer reading
- Declared export value in CAD
- Exporter name and address
- Consignee / importer name and address
- Country of destination
- Planned port of exit
- Planned crossing date and approximate time
- Mode of transport

**Port of exit is a hard commitment.** Switching ports after submission requires amending or re-filing — both possible but they reset the 72-hour clock.

#### 3. Physical evidence at the CBSA booth

- Original Vehicle Registration Certificate, signed over to the buyer
- Notarized Bill of Sale
- PPR / provincial lien search printout
- Photo ID
- CERS proof-of-report number / printout

## Required Document Set (Export Side — Both Paths)

CBSA expects, at minimum:

| Document | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vehicle title or provincial registration** (original, transferred to buyer) | Provincial registry | Most provinces issue a Certificate of Registration. Original registration + Bill of Sale documents ownership. |
| **Bill of Sale** | Buyer + seller | Should include VIN, sale price (CAD), date, full names/addresses, signatures, odometer. **Notarization recommended** — some CBP officers expect it on the US side. |
| **Lien release** (if any lien previously recorded) | Lender / provincial PPR search printout | Vehicle cannot be exported with an active lien. PPR search demonstrates clear title. |
| **Photo ID** | Driver's license / passport | Both buyer and seller IDs at sale; only buyer's needed at border. |
| **CERS proof-of-report** (non-US destinations only) | CERS portal printout / email | Not required for US-bound exports under ¶ 20. |
| **D20-1-1 ¶ 20 binder reference** (US-bound) | Printed excerpt (`12`) | Carry in case officer is unfamiliar with the no-CERS path. |

## Designated Export Offices

Not every CBSA port handles vehicle export. Major designated offices for AB/BC → WA/MT/ID:

- **Coutts, AB / Sweetgrass, MT** — high-volume crossing; vehicle export handled
- **Pacific Highway, BC / Blaine, WA (Truck Crossing)** — preferred for commercial-style vehicle moves
- **Aldergrove, BC / Lynden, WA**
- **Sumas, BC / Sumas, WA**
- **Osoyoos, BC / Oroville, WA**

**Always verify** the chosen port handles vehicle export at the time of travel — call the port's CBSA office. Hours vary; some ports do not handle vehicle export on weekends.

## Provincial Considerations

Each Canadian province has its own export wrinkles. See `alberta-washington-route.md` for AB-specific details (PPR lien search, no PST, registration-vs-title quirk).

## Costs (Export Side)

Generally **no CBSA fees** for personal-use vehicle export. Costs that may apply:

- Provincial lien search (CAD ~$10-50)
- Notary for Bill of Sale (CAD ~$20-100)
- GST: Private sales between individuals are generally not GST-able. Dealer sales to non-residents for export may be zero-rated or eligible for rebate via CRA Form GST189 — confirm with seller and CRA.
- Optional: customs broker (CAD ~$100-300); not required for personal vehicle export.

## Timing — Export Day (US-Bound)

1. **T-day**: Arrive at the designated CBSA port during business hours **before** crossing into the US
2. **CBSA booth**: Have documents identifying the VIN available; present on request. Officer stamps or surrenders registration; you keep the stamped copy (if stamped)
3. **Drive across to CBP booth** on the US side and proceed to import (see `cbp-import.md`)

If the user goes to CBP first without stopping at CBSA, CBP will turn them around. CBSA must release the vehicle from Canada first — even though no declaration is pre-filed for a US-bound export.
