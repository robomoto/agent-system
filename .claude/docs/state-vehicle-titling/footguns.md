# State Vehicle Titling Footguns

## 1. Showing Up at DMV Without the CBP-Stamped HS-7

**Problem**: The vehicle was imported, but the user didn't get HS-7 stamped at the border, or lost the original. State DMV won't title without it. Replacing it requires a written request to the CBP port of entry, sometimes weeks of delay, sometimes refused.

**Fix**: At the border, **insist** the CBP officer stamps HS-7 and EPA 3520-1 and returns the originals. Photograph them at the booth. Store the originals in a folder, not loose in the vehicle.

## 2. Driving Home Without a Trip Permit

**Problem**: Cross the border, drive away, get pulled over without registration or trip permit. State trooper writes a citation; in some states the vehicle can be impounded.

**Fix**: Buy the trip permit online or at a vendor BEFORE crossing the border. WA: dol.wa.gov, $30, 3-day. Schedule everything to fit the window.

## 3. Underdeclaring Purchase Price for Use Tax

**Problem**: Buyer declares $5K on the Bill of Sale to reduce WA use tax on a car worth $30K. DOL clerk pulls up Hagerty/NADA, declares the FMV is $30K, charges use tax on $30K anyway. Now the Bill of Sale is on file showing fraud, and the user has tax fraud exposure.

**Fix**: Declare the actual purchase price. If buying below market (e.g., from a family friend), be ready to pay use tax on FMV anyway and don't fight it. The savings of underdeclaring (~10% × delta) aren't worth the audit risk.

## 4. Assuming Federal 25-Year Exemption Means No State Inspection

**Problem**: Buyer brings a 1968 vehicle into a safety-inspection state (PA, VA, NY) and assumes the FMVSS exemption applies to state inspection. It doesn't — state safety inspection is independent. Vehicle fails on inoperative turn signals, brake pads, etc.

**Fix**: For inspection states, do a pre-trip safety check on the vehicle in Canada or have a planned shop visit before the inspection appointment.

## 5. Choosing Antique Registration Then Daily-Driving the Vehicle

**Problem**: Buyer takes WA Collector Vehicle plates ($35 once, no annual renewal) for the savings, then drives the car to work daily. WSP officer notices, writes a citation under RCW 46.18.220 misuse provisions. Possible: forced switch to standard registration, fines, back-fees.

**Fix**: Be honest about intended use. Daily driver: standard registration. Weekend/show only: collector. The use restriction is real, even if enforcement is sporadic.

## 6. VIN Inspection Expires Before DMV Appointment

**Problem**: WSP inspects the VIN on Day 1, gives a certificate valid 90 days. Buyer waits 100 days to schedule DMV. Inspection has expired; redo at full cost.

**Fix**: Schedule the DMV title appointment within 30 days of the VIN inspection. Most states have similar windows; treat the inspection certificate as time-limited.

## 7. Using a P.O. Box for Residency

**Problem**: WA (and most states) require **proof of residency** for titling — a physical address, not a P.O. Box. Buyer with a P.O. Box gets turned away.

**Fix**: Use a physical residential address. Driver's licence with that address is the cleanest proof; utility bill or lease as backup.

## 8. Not Bringing Cash for Some Border / Inspection Fees

**Problem**: Some smaller WSP inspection stations and rural DMV offices accept only cash or check, not credit cards. Buyer arrives with credit card only, is turned away.

**Fix**: Bring a check or cash for state-side fees. CBP at major ports accepts credit cards; smaller ports don't.

## 9. Forgetting About RTA Tax in King/Pierce/Snohomish (WA)

**Problem**: Buyer in Seattle assumes "use tax" is the only state-side tax and budgets accordingly. RTA tax (1.1% of MSRP-derived value) adds another line. For modern cars this is significant; for a 1968 it's mostly absorbed by the depreciation floor but still adds ~$30-50.

**Fix**: For King/Pierce/Snohomish addresses, include RTA in the cost projection. Other counties: not applicable.

## 10. Plates Don't Arrive in Time for the Drive Home

**Problem**: Some states mail plates after the title appointment instead of issuing them in person. The user has the title in hand but no plate to attach. The trip permit covers the drive home from DMV; after that, the user has a registered car with no plate to display.

**Fix**: WA issues plates same-day at most DOL offices. Other states (NY, NJ, others) mail plates and provide a temporary tag — confirm the issuing pattern at the user's specific DMV office.

## 11. Insurance Bound Before VIN Inspection Reveals VIN Discrepancy

**Problem**: Buyer binds insurance based on the VIN on the Bill of Sale. WSP inspection finds the VIN on the dash differs (transcription error, replaced tag, mismatched paperwork). Insurance is now on a non-existent VIN; underwriter rejects coverage.

**Fix**: Confirm the VIN matches between the foreign title, dash plate, cowl tag, and Bill of Sale BEFORE driving the vehicle to the border. If there's a discrepancy, resolve in Canada (with seller's help) before export.

## 12. Buying Out-of-State to Dodge Use Tax

**Problem**: Buyer titles in Oregon (no sales/use tax) using a friend's address, then garages and drives the car in Washington. WA DOR has audit programs targeting exactly this. If caught, owes back use tax + penalties + interest.

**Fix**: Title where you genuinely live. Tax-arbitrage residency is a high-risk strategy and not the right work for this agent system to facilitate.
