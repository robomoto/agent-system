# State Vehicle Titling — Common Patterns Across States

Patterns shared by most US states. Always confirm against state-specific docs (`washington.md`, etc.) before relying on these for a particular state.

## 1. Federal-State Document Handoff

Every state requires the **CBP-stamped originals** of:
- HS-7 (DOT Declaration)
- EPA 3520-1
- Form 7501 (Entry Summary)

Without these, no state will title an imported vehicle. The user keeps the originals at the border; CBP keeps copies. Lose the originals and the user must request replacements from the port of entry — slow, sometimes refused.

## 2. Foreign Title vs. State Title

Most states recognize a Canadian provincial registration certificate as a "foreign title" for titling purposes. The certificate is surrendered or held for cancellation by the state DMV at the time the new state title is issued. The vehicle then has a **clean state title**.

A few states (varies) brand the title "FOREIGN" or note "PREVIOUSLY REGISTERED IN [country]" — cosmetic for the most part, sometimes affects resale slightly.

## 3. VIN Inspection — Required by Most States

A physical VIN inspection is required in most states for any vehicle without a prior US title. Done by:
- State patrol / state police (CA, WA, OR, etc.)
- DMV-authorized stations (NY, TX)
- Licensed inspectors (variable)

Fee range: $20-100. The inspection verifies the VIN on the vehicle matches the foreign title, looks for signs of tampering, and confirms vehicle identity.

## 4. Use Tax = Sales Tax in Disguise

**Pattern**: Every state with a sales tax has a **use tax** for goods purchased outside the state and brought in. Rate matches the sales tax. Taxable basis is purchase price OR fair market value, whichever is greater. Paid at title/registration time.

Five US states with no statewide sales tax (and thus no use tax on vehicles): Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, Oregon. Buyers who can title in those states avoid the largest single import cost — but most states require **bona fide residency** to title there. Establishing fake residency to dodge use tax is a common scheme and **commonly prosecuted**.

## 5. Tax Credit for Tax Paid in Another Jurisdiction

Most states credit sales/use tax paid in another **US state** but **do not credit foreign tax** (Canadian GST/PST, Mexican IVA). Verify state-by-state — Oregon, Texas, and a few others have specific rules.

For Alberta imports: zero PST in AB, so there's nothing to credit either way.

## 6. Trip Permit / Transit Plate

Every state has a mechanism to drive an unregistered vehicle for a short window. Names vary:

| State | Name | Validity | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| WA | Trip Permit | 3 days | $30 |
| OR | Trip Permit | 21 days | $30-50 |
| CA | One-trip Permit / Temporary Operating Permit | varies | varies |
| TX | 30-day Temporary Permit | 30 days | $25 |
| FL | Temporary Tag | 10/30/60/90 days | varies |

**Universal pitfall**: most must be obtained **before** driving on state roads. Plan for this in advance.

## 7. Antique / Classic / Collector Registration

Most states offer a special registration class for older vehicles. Common thresholds:

| Threshold | States |
|---|---|
| 25 years | OR, CO, several others |
| 30 years | WA, CA, FL, TX, many others |
| 35-40 years | A few northeastern states |

**Common features**:
- Reduced or one-time fee (vs. annual renewal)
- Use restrictions ("collector use", "exhibitions and parades")
- Year-of-manufacture plate option
- Sometimes exempt from emissions testing (academic — most states already exempt pre-1975 vehicles)

The buyer chooses standard vs. collector registration based on intended use. Daily-driver = standard. Weekend/show = collector.

## 8. Insurance at Registration (Not at Title)

A pattern: title can issue without insurance, but **registration** requires proof of insurance. Most buyers do title + registration in the same appointment, so insurance must be ready. The chicken-and-egg of "no plates → can't insure / no insurance → can't register" is solved by binding a policy on the VIN before the appointment — most carriers will write a policy with VIN-only and add the plate number afterward.

## 9. Odometer Disclosure

Federal odometer disclosure rules (49 CFR 580) exempt vehicles **10+ model years old** from federal disclosure. Most states mirror this. For a 1968 vehicle in 2026: exempt federally; some states still want a state-form notation marking "exempt." Don't skip the state form just because federal exemption applies.

## 10. Inspection vs. No Inspection States

Some states require periodic safety inspection (PA, NY, TX, MA, VA, others). Most do not. For an imported vehicle in a safety-inspection state, the **first inspection** is the gate — confirm the vehicle passes the state's standards (working lights, brakes, horn, mirrors, seat belts if originally equipped). Federal 25-year FMVSS exemption does NOT exempt the vehicle from state safety inspection — it only exempts from federal FMVSS conformance.

WA, OR, CA, FL, AZ, NV: no periodic safety inspection.
